# Burrow Brand & Logo Design Context Handoff

## Purpose

This document preserves the thinking behind Burrow's visual identity so future logo, mascot, illustration, UI, website, and campaign explorations can start from the same context instead of re-deriving the philosophy.

## 1. Brand philosophy

Burrow is a local-first, cloud-optional data ownership and recovery layer. It continuously copies critical business data into storage the customer controls, verifies that the copy is recoverable, and provides a unified interface for search, restore, and portability.

The logo should therefore not merely illustrate a rabbit, cave, or backup box. It should make the underlying promise intuitive:

> **Important things have a safe place to live, remain yours, and can be brought back when needed.**

### Emotional territory

- Safe without feeling paranoid
- Reliable without looking corporate or sterile
- Playful without becoming childish
- Technical without looking like infrastructure tooling
- Ownable without looking proprietary or locked-in
- Calm rather than fear-driven backup/security branding

The name Burrow provides the central metaphor: a private, protected place where something valuable is kept close and can still be accessed.

## 2. What the logo must communicate

### Primary meanings

1. Burrow gives important data a safer home.
2. The data is kept independently from the SaaS provider.
3. The system is always there when recovery is needed.
4. Burrow works quietly in the background.
5. The customer remains in control of where the copy lives.

### Secondary meanings

- Curiosity: inspect, dig, discover, understand.
- Persistence: continuous background work.
- Protection: underground shelter.
- Portability: the mascot can appear anywhere.
- Friendliness: make intimidating reliability infrastructure approachable.

The identity must remain credible if Burrow expands from email into Drive, Microsoft 365, Slack, GitHub, databases, documents, and other SaaS data.

## 3. Character / mascot philosophy

The mascot is not separate from the logo. It is the logo's expandable personality layer.

The ideal character:

- Compact, recognizable silhouette
- Simple face
- Expressive paws/hands
- Body that can emerge from a burrow
- Can carry objects and interact with UI concepts
- Friendly and curious, not hyperactive
- Not dependent on realistic animal anatomy
- Not a children's-cartoon character

### Useful expressions / poses

- Peek → discovery/onboarding
- Curious → search/inspection
- Digging → syncing/ingestion
- Carrying → moving data
- Working → background jobs
- Relaxing → healthy/nothing to do
- Celebrating → successful backup/recovery
- Concerned → attention needed

The mascot should become a product UI language, not just marketing art.

## 4. Visual language

Preferred direction:

> **Flat 2D illustration built from simple polygons and organic shapes.**

Dimensionality should come from layered planes and overlap, not glossy 3D rendering.

### Shape rules

- Flat polygons and clean organic curves
- Small number of planes
- No gradients as the primary depth mechanism
- No realistic fur or photographic texture
- Deliberate negative space
- Slight asymmetry to keep the identity alive

The same geometry should work for favicon, app icon, logo, website mascot, onboarding, and campaign illustrations.

### Reference palette

- Deep brown `#2E1F1A`
- Earth brown `#8B5E3C`
- Warm sand `#D9B895`
- Soft cream `#F7EFE7`
- Muted green `#6BA67A`

## 5. Typography

Typography should counterbalance the softness of the mascot.

Preferred:

- Modern geometric or humanist sans-serif
- Strong lowercase treatment
- Confident + approachable
- Avoid overly futuristic fonts
- Avoid handwritten typography for the core wordmark
- Handwritten/sketch typography can exist as a secondary campaign layer

The mascot must work without the wordmark, and the wordmark must work without the mascot.

## 6. Decisions and rejected directions

| Direction | Assessment | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Literal rabbit/rodent mascot | Strong name recognition and warmth, but can become generic | Keep animal concept, refine character |
| Tunnel/cave icon | Good shelter metaphor but too abstract/generic | Do not use as primary mark |
| Shield/lock | Immediately says security but creates cybersecurity/fear positioning | Reject |
| Database/server icon | Technically descriptive but cold and limiting | Reject |
| Pure geometric B | Clean/scalable but loses the human story | Only hidden structural inspiration |
| Cute cartoon animal | Approachable but risks looking like a kids/game brand | Reject exaggerated cuteness |
| Flat polygonal animal | Balances playfulness, technical credibility, scalability and mascot potential | Preferred |

## 7. Product → visual metaphor

| Product idea | Visual expression |
|---|---|
| Continuous sync | Digging / active movement |
| Protected storage | Burrow / underground home |
| Independent copy | Nested or separated object / safe chamber |
| Verification | Curious inspection |
| Recovery | Emerging from burrow / retrieving something |
| Customer-owned storage | A home that belongs to the character |
| Healthy system | Relaxed / sleeping mascot |
| Attention needed | Subtle concerned expression |
| Successful restore | Celebration |
| Search | Curious / looking around |
| Portability | Carrying a box/object |

## 8. Reusable image-generation context

**Brand:** Burrow

**Product:** local-first, cloud-optional SaaS data ownership and recovery layer. Burrow continuously copies critical business data into storage the customer controls, verifies recoverability, and provides search, restore and portability.

**Brand metaphor:** a safe burrow where valuable things are kept, protected and available when needed.

**Personality:** curious, reliable, calm, playful, quietly competent, always there.

**Visual style:** flat 2D illustration built from clean polygons and simple organic shapes. Dimensionality comes from layered planes and overlap, not photorealism or glossy 3D effects.

**Character:** compact friendly burrowing animal, loosely inspired by a small rodent/marmot-like creature, with a simple face, expressive paws and a silhouette that works as a logo. It should feel like infrastructure with a personality, not a children's cartoon.

**Core idea:** protected independent copy.

**Must support:** standalone logo mark, wordmark, favicon/app icon, and larger mascot system with multiple poses and expressions.

**Avoid:** shields, locks, database/server icons, generic cloud icons, crypto aesthetics, excessive gradients, photorealistic animals, childish cartoon styling, overly complex line art, generic tech mascots.

### Prompting principle

Do not simply ask a generator to "make a Burrow logo." Provide the brand meaning, visual constraints, and intended mascot system. Otherwise generators tend to optimize for the literal dictionary meaning of the name.

## 9. Logo and mascot rules

### Primary mark

- Recognizable in silhouette alone
- Works in monochrome
- Does not depend on tiny facial details
- Legible at favicon size
- Does not require the full environment

### Mascot expansion

- Same head/body proportions across poses
- Same eye/facial language
- Props communicate product states
- Environmental scenes can include soil, plants, boxes, files, screens and storage objects
- Calm, understated expressions

### Future animation

- Digging for syncing
- Peek-out for notifications
- Carrying for transfer
- Dust/soil movement for activity
- Subtle celebration for successful recovery

## 10. Future review checklist

Every future visual should answer:

- Does this communicate Burrow rather than generic backup software?
- Is it playful without becoming childish?
- Can the mark become a mascot naturally?
- Does it still work if Burrow protects many SaaS data types?
- Does it communicate safety without cybersecurity clichés?
- Is the silhouette recognizable at small sizes?
- Does it work in monochrome?
- Are the shapes simple enough to reproduce consistently?
- Can the character support at least 8 useful product poses?
- Would it look credible on a technical product website?
- Is it distinctive enough to build a brand around?
- Does every decorative detail serve a purpose?

### Approval threshold

**Target ≥ 9.7/10.**

A concept that is beautiful but generic fails. A concept that communicates the product perfectly but looks ugly or generic also fails.

The goal is:

> **meaning × distinctiveness × scalability × personality**

## 11. Current state

Preferred direction:

**Flat polygonal burrowing creature with a strong compact silhouette, warm earth palette, confident lowercase wordmark, and mascot system built from poses and props.**

The current reference board demonstrates:

- Full and compact logo lockups
- Monochrome treatment
- App-icon direction
- Multiple mascot poses
- Earth/cream/green palette
- Friendly confident typography
- Simple environmental scenes

### What to improve next

- Make the creature silhouette more distinctive and ownable.
- Reduce detail that disappears at small sizes.
- Ensure the logo mark works independently from the wordmark.
- Strengthen the visual relationship between the animal and protected independent copy.
- Create a consistent geometry system for future poses.
- Increase playfulness slightly while retaining technical credibility.

Do not restart from zero unless a new direction clearly beats the current one on meaning, scalability and mascot potential.
